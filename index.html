About

Building galaxies and black holes from the bottom up

In a hierarchical universe, big things are assembled from small ones. Dwarf galaxies were the most common galaxies in the early Universe, and their mergers and interactions are where massive galaxies and the first supermassive black holes began to grow. I study that process at its low-mass end, where it can still be observed directly in the nearby and intermediate-redshift Universe.

Much of my work asks how black holes grow when galaxies meet. I look for intermediate-mass black holes and faint AGN in dwarf galaxies, in dwarf–dwarf pairs and groups, and in the smaller partner of minor mergers, where simulations predict brief bursts of rapid growth. These systems connect the seed black holes of the early Universe to the supermassive black holes we see today.

I also follow what interactions do to the galaxies themselves: when they trigger star formation, when they bury it in dust, and when they shut it down, and whether groups of dwarfs are bound structures on their way to building something larger. To reach the faintest black holes at cosmic noon, I use gravitational lensing by galaxy clusters to magnify sources that X-ray surveys would otherwise miss.

My work combines X-ray, optical and infrared data, mainly from Chandra, Hubble, JWST and Gemini, together with large-scale spectroscopic surveys.

Research

Selected results

Gravitational lensing

A lensed low-luminosity AGN at cosmic noon

Idea
Use a galaxy-cluster lens to magnify a faint high-redshift black hole beyond what X-ray surveys can reach.
Result
A triply imaged source at z ≈ 2.14, magnified ~78×. Chandra detects all three images; the intrinsic LX ≈ 1.2×1042 erg s−1 in a host of log M★ ≈ 8.6–9.4.
Takeaway
A strongly lensed, low-luminosity AGN in a low-mass galaxy at cosmic noon.
A&A, acceptedarXiv:2610.10244
Dwarf groups · hierarchical assembly

The largest catalog of dwarf galaxy groups

Idea
Are groups of dwarf galaxies real, bound systems or short-lived alignments?
Result
74 isolated DESI groups of 3–6 dwarfs (σ ≈ 53 km s−1, crossing time ≈ 0.37 Gyr). Stars alone can't bind them; with dark matter, 69 of 74 favor being bound.
Takeaway
Many are caught mid-assembly: low-mass sites of ongoing hierarchical growth.
ApJ, acceptedarXiv:2610.02587
Dwarf galaxy interactions · galaxy evolution

DESI Interacting Dwarfs Survey

Idea
Measure how dwarf–dwarf interactions change star formation across stellar mass (log M★ 6.5–9.5), redshift (out to z ≈ 1.5) and external tidal environment.
Result
Interactions trigger star formation at intermediate separations. Below ~10 kpc the paths split: isolated pairs become dust-obscured, pairs in weak tidal fields keep forming stars in the open, and pairs in strong tidal fields quench rapidly.
Takeaway
Dwarf interactions have no single evolutionary pathway, with consequences for dusty starbursts, abrupt quenching and compact star-forming systems.
ApJ, accepted · with S. KavirajarXiv:2610.02583
Intermediate-mass black holes

Feeding the Little Monster

Idea
Black holes in the smaller galaxy of a minor merger should go through episodes of rapid growth.
Result
A supersoft X-ray source in the log M★ = 8.24 secondary of a 10:1 merger at z = 0.216, seen in 6.6 Ms of Chandra data. Its thin-disk spectrum implies M• ≈ 800–5,000 M☉ accreting at 20–50% of Eddington.
Takeaway
Minor interactions can grow intermediate-mass black holes toward supermassive ones.
ApJ 1010, 159 (2026) · with J. St. PierrearXiv:2609.02667
Overmassive black holes

Minor merger, major growth

Idea
Theory predicts that minor mergers drive rapid black hole growth in the smaller companion.
Result
A spectroscopically confirmed 35:1 merger at z = 1.824 with JWST tidal features. The secondary hosts an AGN with Lbol ~ (3–7)×1045 erg s−1, requiring a black hole more than 10× above expectations.
Takeaway
Direct observational support for rapid growth of secondary black holes.
ApJ 1004, 228 (2026)arXiv:2605.23844
Interaction-triggered accretion

Low-mass galaxy interactions trigger black hole accretion

Idea
If the early Universe was dominated by dwarfs, do dwarf–dwarf interactions feed their black holes?
Result
82 dwarf pairs and 11 dwarf groups found with Hubble, doubling known samples. Chandra revealed six new AGN (one known before, now seven), and interacting dwarfs host AGN significantly more often than isolated ones.
Takeaway
A dwarf neighbor is efficient at triggering accretion, a channel for growing the first supermassive black holes.
ApJL 968, L21 (2024)arXiv:2405.18685Nature
Dual AGN

The first dual AGN candidates in dwarf–dwarf mergers

Idea
Dual AGN trace the hierarchical assembly of black holes, but none were known in mergers of two dwarfs.
Result
Two candidates with dual X-ray nuclei, tidal tails and bridges, and AGN-like infrared colors. Also SDSS J125417.98+274004.6, an 11:1 minor merger whose secondary AGN matches or outshines the primary.
Takeaway
A first direct look at black hole pairing at the lowest galaxy masses.
ApJ 944, 160 (2023)arXiv:2211.04609OJAp 7 (2024)arXiv:2310.09945NASA · Sci. Am.
